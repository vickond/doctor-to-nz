Chapter 4 — Supplement

🩺 Medical Examination for Your Visa

A detail most guides miss — how to find approved doctors in your country and get results sent directly to Immigration NZ.

What You Need

If you are staying in NZ for more than 12 months, you need two medical documents:

General Medical CertificateFull health examination by an INZ-approved doctor. Confirms you are fit to live and work in NZ.
Chest X-ray CertificateTuberculosis screening. Required for applicants from countries with higher TB rates (including Russia, Ukraine, Israel).
Students (under 12 months)General Medical Certificate not required — only chest X-ray if applicable.
ValidityMedical certificates are valid for 3 months from the date of examination. Time your appointment carefully.

INZ-Approved Doctors — Find One in Your Country

Immigration NZ maintains a list of approved panel physicians in each country. Results from approved doctors are sent directly and electronically to Immigration NZ — you do not need to handle the physical documents yourself.

💡 Your Personal Reference Number

After your examination, ask the doctor for your personal eMedical reference number. Include this number in your visa application. This links your medical results directly to your file in Immigration NZ's system.

⚠️ Approved Panel Physicians Exist in These Countries

Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Georgia, Israel and most other countries have INZ-approved panel physicians. If your country is not on the list, you can use any registered medical facility — but results will not be sent electronically.

⚠️ Important — Studying Medicine from Scratch in NZ

If you are a qualified doctor and want to study medicine again from scratch at a NZ university — your overseas medical degree is generally NOT recognised toward NZ medical programmes. You would need to start from year 2 at best, meaning 5+ more years of full medical study. This is almost never the right path for a qualified specialist. VOC3 pathway is your correct route.

💴 NZ Tax System & KiwiSaver

The most frequently asked questions in immigrant forums — and the most expensive mistakes if you get them wrong.

⚠️ Reality Check for Doctors

As a consultant anaesthesiologist earning NZD 280,000–400,000, the majority of your income is taxed at 39%. This is higher than Israel's top rate of 47% but applies from a lower threshold. Factor this into your financial planning carefully — net take-home after tax is roughly 60–65% of gross salary at senior level.

The 4-Year Transitional Resident Rule — A Major Advantage

As a new tax resident, you may qualify for a 4-year exemption on overseas-sourced income. This means income earned from Israeli investments, rental property, or savings during your first 4 years in NZ is generally not taxed in NZ. This is one of the most overlooked benefits for immigrants — and one of the most valuable.

💡 Get a Tax Accountant in Year 1

NZ does not automatically file taxes for you — you must do it yourself via myIR. A tax accountant familiar with IMG doctors pays for itself many times over. Ask your hospital for referrals.

KiwiSaver — Free Money From Your Hospital

KiwiSaver is NZ's voluntary retirement savings scheme. As a hospital employee, your employer is legally required to contribute at least 3% of your salary on top of your pay. But under the NZRDA (Resident Doctors' Association) collective agreement, NZ public hospitals match up to 6% dollar-for-dollar.

Your contribution3–10% of gross salary (you choose)
Employer contributionMinimum 3%, up to 6% under NZRDA agreement
Government contribution25 cents per dollar you contribute, max NZD 261/year
On NZD 300,000 salaryEmployer adds NZD 18,000/year — free retirement savings
Opt-out windowMust decide within 50 days of starting employment
AccessLocked until age 65 (or first home purchase after 3 years)
💡 Contribute 6% from Day 1

If your hospital matches 6% and you contribute 6%, that's NZD 36,000/year going into your retirement fund on a NZD 300K salary — NZD 18K yours + NZD 18K free from the hospital. Opt-in within 50 days of starting. You cannot retroactively opt in after this window.

Settling In — What Nobody Tells You

🔑 How to Actually Rent a Home in NZ

Finding a rental without NZ credit history is the #1 practical challenge. Here's how to do it.

The NZ Rental Process — Step by Step

1
Search listings on TradeMe, RealEstate.co.nz, or NZ Flatmates

Rent is quoted weekly in NZ — multiply by 4.33 for monthly. Start searching 4-6 weeks before you need the property.

2
Attend an open home or arrange a viewing

Popular rentals in Auckland get 20-30 applications. Be ready to move fast — same-day applications are common.

3
Submit a rental application

Applications require: ID, references (2-3 people), proof of income, rental history. As a new immigrant you won't have NZ rental history — compensate with employer letter and extra references.

4
Pay bond (4 weeks rent) + 2 weeks rent in advance

Bond must be lodged with Tenancy Services (not held by landlord). Total upfront: 6 weeks rent. On a NZD 3,500/month rental that's approx. NZD 4,850.

5
Complete a property condition report

Document all existing damage at move-in with photos. This protects your bond at the end of tenancy. Use the Tenancy Services app.

Getting a Rental Without NZ History — What Works

  • Employer letter: A letter from your hospital confirming your role, start date, and salary is the single most powerful document. Request it before arrival.
  • Offer a larger bond: You can legally offer more than 4 weeks (up to what the landlord accepts) — shows commitment.
  • Bank statements: 3 months of Israeli bank statements showing healthy savings.
  • References from Israel: Previous landlord reference letters (translate to English).
  • Professional references: Letters from colleagues or supervisors describing you as reliable.
  • Temporary housing first: Arrive with 4-6 weeks of Airbnb or serviced apartment booked. This relieves pressure and lets you view properties in person.
⚠️ Rent is Quoted Weekly in NZ

A listing saying "NZD 875/week" means NZD 3,794/month. Always mentally multiply by 4.33 to get the monthly figure. This catches many immigrants off guard.

🚗 Buying a Car in New Zealand

Public transport in Auckland is improving but a car is essential for families. Here's what to know before you buy.

Your Israeli Licence in NZ

First 12 monthsDrive on your Israeli licence — no conversion needed
After 12 monthsMust convert to NZ licence — theory test only (no practical test required for Israeli licence holders)
Where to convertNZTA — visit any licensing agent or AA centre
CostApprox. NZD 70-100 for theory test + licence fee
NZ drives onLeft side of the road ⬅️ — give yourself 2-4 weeks to adapt

How to Buy a Used Car Safely

1
Search TradeMe Motors or AutoTrader

Filter by: make, year, km, price. Most private sales in NZ are on TradeMe.

2
Run a Carjam check (carjam.co.nz)

For NZD 9, get full vehicle history: odometer readings, WoF history, registration status, accident reports, finance owing. Always do this before viewing.

3
Check for outstanding finance (PPSR)

If a car has unpaid finance on it, the lender can repossess it even after you buy it. Check: ppsr.govt.nz

4
Get a pre-purchase inspection

An AA inspection costs approx. NZD 150-200 and covers mechanical condition, safety items, and WoF issues. Worth every dollar.

5
Register the car in your name

At NZTA or online via NZTA's website. You need NZ address and payment. Do this same day as purchase.

WoF — Warrant of Fitness

Every car in NZ must have a current WoF — a safety inspection. Cars under 6 years old need it annually; older cars every 6 months. WoF costs approx. NZD 50-80 and is done at any approved garage or AA centre.

⚠️ Never Drive Without a Current WoF

Driving with an expired WoF is a NZD 200 fine. Always check the WoF expiry sticker on the windscreen when buying a car.

🥝 Culture Shocks & Things That Surprise Immigrants

From hundreds of expat forum posts — what people genuinely did not expect.

The Top Surprises

❄️
Houses are cold inside in winter

Most NZ homes are built from timber with no double glazing and no central heating. Auckland winters reach 8°C outside — and sometimes feel that cold inside. Budget NZD 200-500 for a good heat pump (reverse-cycle air conditioner). This is the #1 complaint of newcomers.

⬅️
Driving on the left

Give yourself 2-4 weeks to fully adapt. The most dangerous moment is turning at intersections — your natural instinct will be wrong. Paste a note on your dashboard: "KEEP LEFT".

🎄
Seasons are reversed

December = summer (25-28°C). June-August = winter (8-15°C). Christmas is at the beach. This takes 1-2 years to feel normal. NZ school year starts in late January.

📏
Everything is far away

NZ is geographically isolated. Auckland to Wellington = 640 km (fly or 8-hour drive). A flight to Sydney = 3 hours. Israel = 20+ hours. Plan annual trips home carefully — they are expensive and exhausting.

😌
"She'll be right" culture

Kiwis are relaxed and informal — sometimes frustratingly so for detail-oriented professionals. Doctors, tradespeople, and suppliers may not respond urgently. Build more time into everything. It is not rudeness — it is the culture.

🏥
GP wait times are long

Many GP clinics are not accepting new patients. Find and enrol with a GP in your first week — before you need one. Enrolled patients pay 30-50% less per visit. Without enrolment, a visit costs NZD 75-130.

🌿
Biosecurity is very strict

NZ takes biosecurity extremely seriously. Do not bring fresh food, soil, plants, or wooden items without declaring them. Fines for non-declaration start at NZD 400. Always declare everything at customs — even if you think it is fine.

🏉
Rugby is a religion

The All Blacks are New Zealand's national identity. Learn a few basics — it helps enormously with social integration, especially with hospital colleagues. You do not need to love the sport, but knowing the game exists and matters is important.

🪶
Te Tiriti o Waitangi — the Treaty matters

New Zealand is founded on a Treaty between the Crown and Māori. As a doctor, you will undergo mandatory cultural safety training. Take it seriously — it is not box-ticking. Understanding Māori health perspectives makes you a better doctor and a better colleague.

⚠️ Health Hazards Unique to New Zealand

☀️ UV RadiationNew Zealand has one of the highest UV radiation levels in the world due to the Antarctic ozone hole. Skin cancer rates are among the highest globally. Danger period: October–April (NZ summer). Always use SPF 50+ sunscreen, wear hats and UV-protective clothing — especially for children.
🦷 Dental CostsDental care is NOT covered by the public health system (except for children under 18). Adult dental costs are high — a filling costs NZD 150–300, a crown NZD 1,500–2,500. Many NZ residents travel to Asia for dental work. Budget NZD 100–300/month for family dental care and get dental cover in your health insurance.
🌊 Rip CurrentsNZ beaches are beautiful but dangerous. Rip currents kill several people each year. Always swim between the red-and-yellow flags at patrolled beaches. Never swim alone or at unpatrolled beaches.
🌿 BiosecurityDeclare EVERYTHING at customs — fresh food, soil, wooden items, plants. Non-declaration fines start at NZD 400. NZ biosecurity officers are thorough and non-negotiable.

🌋 Safety, Earthquakes & Emergency Preparedness

New Zealand sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire. Earthquakes are common. Being prepared is part of life here.

Earthquake Basics

FrequencyNZ has ~15,000 earthquakes per year. Most are too small to feel.
Auckland riskLower than Wellington or Christchurch — but not zero. Auckland also sits on 53 volcanoes.
Wellington riskHigh — major fault line runs through the city. Built for it — buildings are engineered.
ChristchurchRebuilt post-2011 earthquake. Modern buildings are very safe.
Timber housesNZ timber homes actually perform better in earthquakes than brick/concrete — they flex rather than crumble.

What to Do in an Earthquake

  • Drop, Cover, Hold: Drop to hands and knees, take cover under a sturdy table or desk, hold on until shaking stops
  • If outside: Move away from buildings, power lines, trees — drop to the ground and cover your head
  • If driving: Pull over away from bridges and overpasses, stay in the car
  • After shaking stops: Check for gas leaks, structural damage. Expect aftershocks.
  • Tsunami warning: If near coast and shaking is long/strong — move to high ground immediately, do not wait for official warning

Emergency Kit — Keep This at Home

  • Water: 3 litres per person per day, for at least 3 days
  • Food: Non-perishable items for 3 days (family of 4 = significant supply)
  • First aid kit and any regular medications (2-week supply)
  • Torch, batteries, portable phone charger
  • Cash (ATMs may not work after disaster)
  • Copies of important documents (passports, insurance, medical records)
  • Warm clothes and blankets

⚠️ Home Insurance — Always Include Earthquake Cover

Standard NZ contents insurance includes EQC levy (earthquake commission). But you should verify your policy explicitly covers natural disasters. After the Christchurch earthquakes, many uninsured homeowners lost everything. As a renter, contents insurance is essential — the landlord's insurance covers the building only, not your belongings.
